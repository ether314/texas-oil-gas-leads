        How to use this site, and what the columns mean

        How to use it

        • Map shades counties by permits, production, jobs, establishments, mineral value or companies, and shows permit locations, refineries, gas plants, LNG terminals and where the pooled companies sit. Pick a basin to frame it, press Watch Texas drill to replay two years of permits, or click a county for its numbers.
        • Operators lists every active Railroad Commission registrant that is a corporation, LLC or limited partnership. Start with bands A and B: they are drilling now. Tick Top producers to see who produces the most oil.
        • Companies lists the Comptroller pool: entities filed under an oil and gas industry code, plus entities with no code whose names say oil and gas. Where a company matched an RRC operator, its permit counts are filled in.
        • On this page, click a bar, a number or a market figure to open the matching records or chart.
        • Share this view on the Map and on both tables copies a link that reopens exactly what you see. Download CSV exports the rows you filtered, public fields only.

        Reading the columns

        Band A, B, C
        Drilling activity from RRC permits. A: 10 or more new-well permits in the last 12 months. B: 1 to 9. C: an RRC operator with none. Companies with no RRC match show a dash.
        Trend
        New-drill permits in the last 12 full calendar months against the 12 before. "new" means none in the prior 12. Blank means the operator has no permit in the last 24 months.
        Oil, gas, production trend
        Crude oil plus condensate (barrels) and gas (mcf) reported to the RRC over the latest 12 months, and the change against the 12 months before. Blank means no production figure for that operator, not zero.
        Spud, depth
        Spud counts new-drill permits whose reported spud date falls in the last 12 months. Depth is the mean reported total depth of the operator's permits over 24 months.
        Fit
        Likely is an active entity with no warning signs. Check means verify first: a mineral or royalty name, six or more entities at one address, a weak name match (drilling, pipeline, well service), or a fuel wholesaler.
        New entity
        Chartered with the Texas Secretary of State in the last 24 months.
        New to drilling, New P-5
        First drilling permit ever filed, or first RRC registration, in the last 24 months.
        RRC match
        How a company was tied to an RRC operator: exact name, name prefix (RRC names are cut at 32 characters), or a similar name in the same ZIP, which is the least reliable.
        New wells, permits
        Permits are counted once per permit number. New wells counts new-drill applications only; recompletions and re-entries count under permits.

        What is not on this site

        Officer names, phone numbers and street addresses stay in the CSV files, not here. Sole proprietors and other non-entity registrants, trust and family names, and deprioritized names are left out.

          Sources & method

          Data used

          Every series ends on its own date; the freshness list on the Overview shows each one. The tag on a source says how often that source publishes. This site is rebuilt by hand (last built ), so it can lag. Sources without a tag have no documented schedule here.

          Drilling and operators

          • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, P-5 Organization file. Every registered operator and its activity roles. File refreshed .
          • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer. Operators and permit issue dates since 1976. File refreshed ; the newest permit in it was issued .
          • Publishes monthly and nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer (includes latitudes and longitudes). The surface location of each permit, read from the month-end files for October 2024 to September 2026 plus the current daily file. Coordinates are NAD27 and were not converted to WGS84, an offset of tens of meters that is invisible at map scale.
          • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, Production Data Query dump (PDQ_DSV.zip, 3.45 GB, extracted October 20, 2026, production months 1993-01 to 2026-08), read table by table without unpacking it: monthly volumes by county, by lease and county (77 million rows) and by operator. Crude oil plus condensate is in barrels and gas-well plus casinghead gas in mcf (shown here as Bcf, a billion cubic feet). Added up by county, by operator of record and for the state.
          • Production stops at January 2026. The Railroad Commission counts only the reports it has received, so its newest months are far from complete: statewide oil for July 2026 is 10.8% below the EIA's estimate, against 1.1% below for January 2026. The production views end at the last month where the RRC is within 2% of the EIA, and the months after it (February to August 2026) are never charted or ranked. Through June 2025 the two sources agree to 0.03% on average, and statewide gas matches the EIA's gross withdrawals within 2.4% through March 2026. Permits run to October 2026, so a permit figure and a production figure on the same screen cover different windows; each is labeled with its own. County volumes are the RRC's allocation of leases that cross county lines, which its manual calls an estimate. An operator's volumes follow the operator of record month by month, so a jump is often an acquisition rather than drilling.
          • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, P-5 Organization file (orf850.txt.gz, 19.5 MB, 78,403 organizations). Used to name the operators in the production files: an operator is named when its organization type is corporation, LLC or limited partnership, whatever its P-5 status, which keeps individuals and sole proprietors out. Operators whose registration is not current (status other than A) carry a small tag and are not on the Operators page, which lists active registrants only. Their volumes still count in every county and state figure.

          Companies and places

          • Texas Comptroller, Active Franchise Taxpayers on data.texas.gov. About 3.5 million active entities with name, city, ZIP, formation date and a self-reported NAICS industry code. Updated .
          • U.S. Census Bureau, cartographic boundary files. The 254 Texas county outlines (2025, 1:5,000,000, clipped to the shoreline).
          • U.S. Census Bureau, ZIP Code Tabulation Area Gazetteer (2025). The center point used to place each ZIP code on the map.
          • Publishes annually Bureau of Labor Statistics, Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages. 2025 annual averages by county for oil and gas extraction (NAICS 211) and for support activities for mining (NAICS 213, the oilfield services industry), private ownership: establishments, jobs and average weekly wage.
          • Publishes annually U.S. Census Bureau, County Business Patterns 2023, the bulk county file cbp23co.zip (12.9 MB; 74,769 of its 1.1 million rows are Texas). Establishments with payroll in March 2023 by county for oil and gas extraction (211), support activities for mining (213), pipeline construction (237120), pipeline transportation (486), petroleum wholesale (4247) and oilfield machinery manufacturing (333132). 2023 is the newest vintage; the 2024 file is not out yet. This site uses establishment counts and the size mix only: the Census adds noise to employment, and its NAICS 211 employment is about 64% of the BLS figure, so use the BLS jobs for levels. Size classes with fewer than 3 establishments are withheld by the Census and shown here as none, so a county's classes can add up to less than its total. 179 counties have at least one establishment in these industries; the others have none.

          Prices, outlook, rigs, jobs and taxes

          • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration: WTI crude oil spot price and Henry Hub natural gas spot price (monthly averages), and Texas field production of crude oil (thousand barrels a month), January 2019 onward. Prices run through September 2026 and Texas production through July 2026 (August and September are not yet published); the newest production months are EIA estimates that get revised.
          • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026 edition (modeling completed October 1, 2026; the data workbook STEO_m.xlsx, 1.1 MB, Table 1). WTI and Henry Hub forecasts for October 2026 to December 2027. A forecast, not data: it is drawn as a shaded zone with a lighter line. STEO calls every month through September 2026 history, but the newest survey-based months are probably estimates.
          • Publishes weekly Baker Hughes, North America Rotary Rig Count, weekly counts for Texas, the Permian, Eagle Ford, Haynesville and Barnett from January 4, 2019 to August 28, 2026 (400 weeks). Baker Hughes' own site blocks scripted access (HTTP 403), so the weekly workbooks were read from Internet Archive copies of Baker Hughes' public files: the "New Report (2013-Aug 2025)" workbook (11.8 MB) and the August 28, 2026 report (8.0 MB), both captured August 29, 2026. Rows are summed per publish date; the two workbooks agree on all 87 weeks they share, and the totals match each workbook's own summary sheet. Weeks after August 28, 2026 are missing because later workbooks are not archived. Baker Hughes' Permian basin includes southeastern New Mexico, so this site's "Permian, Texas counties" line uses only the Texas part; its Haynesville count includes Louisiana and its Barnett label is wider than the county list.
          • Publishes monthly Bureau of Labor Statistics, State and Area Employment (CES), Texas all employees in oil and gas extraction (NAICS 211) and support activities for mining (NAICS 213), thousands of jobs, monthly from January 2019 through September 2026, read through the public BLS API (version 1, no key). Not seasonally adjusted (BLS publishes no adjusted series for these two industries). The newest month is a first print that BLS revises.
          • Publishes monthly Texas Comptroller, Revenue Watch, All Funds Historical data (all-funds-historical.xlsx, 129 KB, last modified October 1, 2026): oil production tax and natural gas production tax collected each month from January 2019 through August 2026, in USD millions. Cash basis by month of collection and net of refunds; production is taxed about two months after it happens. September 2026 is not published yet. August 2020 gas is negative because refunds exceeded receipts.
          • Publishes annually Texas Comptroller, School District Property Value Study, 2025 final findings (2025-final-isd-worksheet-export-detail.xlsx, 1.7 MB, certified August 27, 2026). The value assigned to category G (oil, gas and other mineral reserves) in each county, summed over its school districts, before exemptions. Its statewide total equals the Comptroller's own summary to the dollar. 31 counties have none. It is an appraised value, not production or reserves.
          • Basins: counties for the Permian (51 Texas counties), Eagle Ford (23) and Haynesville (13) from the EIA Drilling Productivity Report region lists (dpr-data.xlsx, 156 KB, last modified May 13, 2024), and the Barnett (25 counties) from the Railroad Commission's Barnett Shale page, since EIA has no Barnett region. The RRC lists a few more counties for the Permian, Eagle Ford and Haynesville; those are left out. 92.6% of last-12-month permits fall in these 112 counties.
          • Facilities: refinery capacity from the EIA Refinery Capacity Report (operable capacity on January 1, 2026) and LNG capacity from EIA's U.S. liquefaction capacity file (third quarter 2026). Refinery, gas processing plant and LNG locations come from the EPA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program facility table for reporting year 2023, which is also the source of the gas processing plant list. EIA's Energy Atlas map layers require a token and were not used directly; two coordinates (the Petromax refinery and Golden Pass LNG) come from a copy of EIA's Energy Atlas layer hosted by Natural Resources Canada.

          Daily report

          • Publishes nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer, Daily File (daf420.dat, about 3 MB). The current month's permits, read each night for the Daily report: permits issued by day, application type, county, district, operator and planned depth. The monthly Permit Master above is what the county, operator and company tables are built from, so the two can differ by the days since the Master's last refresh.
          • Publishes twice daily Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permits Pending Approval. W-1 applications submitted and still waiting when the 11 AM and 5 PM sets were written, with the applicant, county, district, planned depth and whether it is flagged to expedite. A report shows the sets that reached the RRC's share that day: the previous evening's 5 PM set, which is copied after midnight, and the day's 11 AM set.
          • Publishes nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Completion Information in Data Format. A zip per day of completion forms (W-2, G-1, P-4, L-1, G-5, G-10, P-15, W-12) submitted or approved that day: counts by district, county and operator, and the best oil tests. The RRC posts a day's zip at about 2:20 the next morning, so a report shows the day before's.
          • Publishes nightly Drilling Permit (W1) Imaged Files, Directional Survey Applications and Imaged Completion Files. Zips of PDF and TIFF images. Only the size of each zip is read, as a rough sign of how busy the day was.
          • The Daily report keeps 92 days. Days before the first nightly capture were rebuilt from the RRC's own file history by the same rule (month-end permit files, and the completion zips, image packs and pending sets that had landed on the share each day) and are marked as reconstructed. Operators are named only when they are corporations, LLCs or limited partnerships; lease names and well numbers are never shown.

          Method

          • Operators: all active P-5 registrants, narrowed to the that are corporations, LLCs or limited partnerships.
          • Companies: entities filed under an oil and gas NAICS code, plus active entities with no code whose names contain an oil and gas word such as oilfield, petroleum or wireline. Name-only matches on drilling, pipeline and well service never rate better than check.
          • RRC matching is by normalized company name, then by name prefix, then by a similar name in the same ZIP with the same leading letters and numerals.
          • New-drill permits are application type 01, counted once per permit number. "Last 12 months" counts run from October 29, 2025 to the newest permit in the RRC file, which is why they cover fewer days than the prior 12 months. Trends and the map's change view therefore compare complete calendar months instead: the last 12 against the 12 before.
          • Map shading: counties are binned into up to seven classes with the same number of counties in each (quantiles); the legend names each class and its unit and year. Counties with no value in a class are shown as "no data" or as zero where the source lists them as none. The change views use fixed bands around zero. A county with fewer than 10 permits in 24 months is not rated for permit change, and one with under 100,000 barrels in 24 months is not rated for production change.
          • Production change compares the latest 12 complete months of oil with the 12 before (for example 12 months to January 2026 against the 12 before). Production figures for counties and operators add up reported lease volumes; an operator's figures are for the leases it operates.
          • Basin cards and the basin outline on the map use the county lists above. The operators named on a basin card come from each county's top-eight operator list, so they are the operators with the most permits there, not a full ranking.
          • Companies by county use the Comptroller's county code, which numbers counties alphabetically (not in FIPS order); 107 Texas rows with code 0 are left out of the county counts. The County filter on the Companies tab uses the county that holds each ZIP code's center, so its counts can differ from the map's (within about 2% for most counties, more for a few such as Midland).
          • Companies by ZIP: 1,560 ZIP codes cover every pooled Texas company. 239 ZIPs have no Census center (PO boxes and the like) and are placed at their city's center, so several ZIPs of one city can share a point.
          • County permit counts, trends and rankings, the market charts and the headline numbers use every permit. Only the dots, and the Watch Texas drill replay, use the located subset. The replay shows each located permit on its issue date, over the last 24 months.
          • The Insights on the Overview are computed in your browser from the same files. Growth compares the last 12 full months with the 12 before, and counties or operators with a small base are left out.

          Limits

          • Series end on different dates. Permits run to October 7, 2026, rig counts to August 28, EIA Texas crude to July, tax receipts to August, jobs and prices to September, and the EIA outlook is a forecast to December 2027. Read each chip and chart against its own date.
          • About 80% of Comptroller entities have no NAICS code. Service companies with generic names, no code and no RRC registration are not in the pool.
          • Permits measure activity, not budget or need.
          • Permit dots are a located subset. The RRC publishes a surface location for 70% of new-drill permits in the last 24 months but only 40% of the last 12, and the gaps are uneven: January 2026 and April to October 2026 are thin, and the busiest counties (Midland, Reeves, Upton, Martin) have almost no dots. Do not read dot density, or the replay, as activity; read the shading and the counts.
          • Seven offshore permits are in the statewide totals but not in any county or dot.
          • Operator trend, spud, depth and the 24-month chart exist for the 504 operators with a permit in the last 24 months. A blank cell means no data, never zero.
          • Production is older than permits. Oil and gas figures end at January 2026, the newest month the Railroad Commission's reports cover completely; permits run to October 2026. Oil and gas from operators that are not current P-5 registrants appear on the Overview only, tagged, since the Operators page lists active registrants.
          • BLS withholds county figures that could identify an employer: 52 counties have oil and gas figures and 101 have services figures, so most counties show "No data (suppressed by BLS)". County jobs figures are for 2025; the monthly jobs chart is statewide.
          • The EIA outlook is a model forecast and will be revised each month. Weekly rig counts after August 28, 2026 are not shown because they could not be read; they are missing, not zero.
          • Gas processing plants carry no capacity (no current public source), some entries are compressor or fractionation sites inside a plant, and facilities built after 2023 are missing. Port Arthur LNG and Rio Grande LNG are under construction and not shown.
          • Fit flags are rules applied to names and addresses, not research. Verify before you reach out.
          • Matching has been spot-checked, not audited.

          Other public sources, not used yet

          • SEC EDGAR: executives of public companies, and Form D filings for private operators raising money.
          • FERC, PHMSA and EPA ECHO: pipelines, power plants and large industrial gas users.
