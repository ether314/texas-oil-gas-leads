    What each permit captures

    The same questions asked of every agency’s drilling permit: who the operator is, where the well is, how deep, what is spilled or drilled through, what was pumped in the fracturing job, and which dates and documents the agency keeps. Each answer says whether the agency publishes it as data, shows it only in a lookup, asks for it only on the form or by rule, or does not capture it at all.

    Companies

    Who is drilling for oil in every state that publishes its permits, ranked by new wells in the last 12 months, and the Texas companies that serve them.

    What is counted

    Daily report

    The new wells of one day, nationwide: fracked wells (filled dots, every state that reports) and oil permits and spuds (rings, where an agency publishes a location) on one map, with which states they are in and how that day compares with a typical one. A fracked well is dated by the day of its first fracturing job; operators disclose weeks to months afterwards, so the newest days are still filling in.

    Texas well images

    Every business day the Railroad Commission of Texas publishes the scanned filings about wells as three packs: the approved permit letters and plats, the directional surveys, and the forms and logs filed when wells are completed. Find any well by its API number and read what the Commission filed about it, then see what a well leaves behind as images, how much arrives each day, and what any day’s packs hold.

    Sources & method

    Data used

    Every series ends on its own date; the freshness list at the foot of the Overview shows the new-wells source and the permit files. The tag on a source says how often that source publishes. This site is rebuilt by hand (last built ), so it can lag. Sources without a tag have no documented schedule here.

    Drilling and operators

    • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, P-5 Organization file. Every registered operator and its activity roles. File refreshed .
    • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer. Operators and permit issue dates since 1976. File refreshed ; the newest permit in it was issued .
    • Publishes monthly and nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer (includes latitudes and longitudes). The surface location of each permit, read from the month-end files for October 2024 to September 2026 plus the current daily file. Coordinates are NAD27 and were not converted to WGS84, an offset of tens of meters that is invisible at map scale.
    • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, Production Data Query dump (PDQ_DSV.zip, 3.45 GB, extracted October 20, 2026, production months 1993-01 to 2026-08), read table by table without unpacking it: monthly volumes by county, by lease and county (77 million rows) and by operator. Crude oil plus condensate is in barrels and gas-well plus casinghead gas in mcf (shown here as Bcf, a billion cubic feet). Added up by county, by operator of record and for the state.
    • Production stops at January 2026. The Railroad Commission counts only the reports it has received, so its newest months are far from complete: statewide oil for July 2026 is 10.8% below the EIA's estimate, against 1.1% below for January 2026. The production views end at the last month where the RRC is within 2% of the EIA, and the months after it (February to August 2026) are never charted or ranked. Through June 2025 the two sources agree to 0.03% on average, and statewide gas matches the EIA's gross withdrawals within 2.4% through March 2026. Permits run to October 2026, so a permit figure and a production figure on the same screen cover different windows; each is labeled with its own. County volumes are the RRC's allocation of leases that cross county lines, which its manual calls an estimate. An operator's volumes follow the operator of record month by month, so a jump is often an acquisition rather than drilling.
    • Publishes monthly Railroad Commission of Texas, P-5 Organization file (orf850.txt.gz, 19.5 MB, 78,403 organizations). Used to name the operators in the production files: an operator is named when its organization type is corporation, LLC or limited partnership, whatever its P-5 status, which keeps individuals and sole proprietors out. Operators whose registration is not current (status other than A) carry a small tag and are not on the Operators page, which lists active registrants only. Their volumes still count in every county and state figure.

    Companies and places

    • Texas Comptroller, Active Franchise Taxpayers on data.texas.gov. About 3.5 million active entities with name, city, ZIP, formation date and a self-reported NAICS industry code. Updated .
    • U.S. Census Bureau, cartographic boundary files. The 254 Texas county outlines (2025, 1:5,000,000, clipped to the shoreline).
    • U.S. Census Bureau, ZIP Code Tabulation Area Gazetteer (2025). The center point used to place each ZIP code on the map.
    • Publishes annually Bureau of Labor Statistics, Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages. 2025 annual averages by county for oil and gas extraction (NAICS 211) and for support activities for mining (NAICS 213, the oilfield services industry), private ownership: establishments, jobs and average weekly wage.
    • Publishes annually U.S. Census Bureau, County Business Patterns 2023, the bulk county file cbp23co.zip (12.9 MB; 74,769 of its 1.1 million rows are Texas). Establishments with payroll in March 2023 by county for oil and gas extraction (211), support activities for mining (213), pipeline construction (237120), pipeline transportation (486), petroleum wholesale (4247) and oilfield machinery manufacturing (333132). 2023 is the newest vintage; the 2024 file is not out yet. This site uses establishment counts and the size mix only: the Census adds noise to employment, and its NAICS 211 employment is about 64% of the BLS figure, so use the BLS jobs for levels. Size classes with fewer than 3 establishments are withheld by the Census and shown here as none, so a county's classes can add up to less than its total. 179 counties have at least one establishment in these industries; the others have none.

    Prices, outlook, rigs, jobs and taxes

    • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration: WTI crude oil spot price and Henry Hub natural gas spot price (monthly averages), and Texas field production of crude oil (thousand barrels a month), January 2019 onward. Prices run through September 2026 and Texas production through July 2026 (August and September are not yet published); the newest production months are EIA estimates that get revised.
    • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook, October 2026 edition (modeling completed October 1, 2026; the data workbook STEO_m.xlsx, 1.1 MB, Table 1). WTI and Henry Hub forecasts for October 2026 to December 2027. A forecast, not data: it is drawn as a shaded zone with a lighter line. STEO calls every month through September 2026 history, but the newest survey-based months are probably estimates.
    • Publishes weekly Baker Hughes, North America Rotary Rig Count, weekly counts for Texas, the Permian, Eagle Ford, Haynesville and Barnett from January 4, 2019 to August 28, 2026 (400 weeks). Baker Hughes' own site blocks scripted access (HTTP 403), so the weekly workbooks were read from Internet Archive copies of Baker Hughes' public files: the "New Report (2013-Aug 2025)" workbook (11.8 MB) and the August 28, 2026 report (8.0 MB), both captured August 29, 2026. Rows are summed per publish date; the two workbooks agree on all 87 weeks they share, and the totals match each workbook's own summary sheet. Weeks after August 28, 2026 are missing because later workbooks are not archived. Baker Hughes' Permian basin includes southeastern New Mexico, so this site's "Permian, Texas counties" line uses only the Texas part; its Haynesville count includes Louisiana and its Barnett label is wider than the county list.
    • Publishes monthly Bureau of Labor Statistics, State and Area Employment (CES), Texas all employees in oil and gas extraction (NAICS 211) and support activities for mining (NAICS 213), thousands of jobs, monthly from January 2019 through September 2026, read through the public BLS API (version 1, no key). Not seasonally adjusted (BLS publishes no adjusted series for these two industries). The newest month is a first print that BLS revises.
    • Publishes monthly Texas Comptroller, Revenue Watch, All Funds Historical data (all-funds-historical.xlsx, 129 KB, last modified October 1, 2026): oil production tax and natural gas production tax collected each month from January 2019 through August 2026, in USD millions. Cash basis by month of collection and net of refunds; production is taxed about two months after it happens. September 2026 is not published yet. August 2020 gas is negative because refunds exceeded receipts.
    • Publishes annually Texas Comptroller, School District Property Value Study, 2025 final findings (2025-final-isd-worksheet-export-detail.xlsx, 1.7 MB, certified August 27, 2026). The value assigned to category G (oil, gas and other mineral reserves) in each county, summed over its school districts, before exemptions. Its statewide total equals the Comptroller's own summary to the dollar. 31 counties have none. It is an appraised value, not production or reserves.
    • Basins: counties for the Permian (51 Texas counties), Eagle Ford (23) and Haynesville (13) from the EIA Drilling Productivity Report region lists (dpr-data.xlsx, 156 KB, last modified May 13, 2024), and the Barnett (25 counties) from the Railroad Commission's Barnett Shale page, since EIA has no Barnett region. The RRC lists a few more counties for the Permian, Eagle Ford and Haynesville; those are left out. 92.6% of last-12-month permits fall in these 112 counties.
    • Facilities: refinery capacity from the EIA Refinery Capacity Report (operable capacity on January 1, 2026) and LNG capacity from EIA's U.S. liquefaction capacity file (third quarter 2026). Refinery, gas processing plant and LNG locations come from the EPA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program facility table for reporting year 2023, which is also the source of the gas processing plant list. EIA's Energy Atlas map layers require a token and were not used directly; two coordinates (the Petromax refinery and Golden Pass LNG) come from a copy of EIA's Energy Atlas layer hosted by Natural Resources Canada.

    Nightly captures, no longer shown in the Daily report

    The Daily report is national now. Its fracked wells are the FracFocus new-wells data (above), for every state; the oil permits and spuds drawn as rings on the same map are the points in the permit files of Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico and the federal offshore (the records that have a location, so only part of Texas’s), and no other state’s, because no other publishes one. The three nightly captures below still run: they are the archive behind the Texas images page and the well finder, and their day files stay published as data. Their pages (the permit queue, completions, transfers, inventories and the drill pages) were dropped from the Daily report because only these states publish them.

    Nightly capture: Texas

    • Publishes nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permit Master and Trailer, Daily File (daf420.dat, about 3 MB). The current month's permits, read each night for the Daily report: permits issued by day, application type, county, district, operator and planned depth. The monthly Permit Master above is what the county, operator and company tables are built from, so the two can differ by the days since the Master's last refresh.
    • Publishes twice daily Railroad Commission of Texas, Drilling Permits Pending Approval. W-1 applications submitted and still waiting when the 11 AM and 5 PM sets were written, with the applicant, county, district, planned depth and whether it is flagged to expedite. A report shows the sets that reached the RRC's share that day: the previous evening's 5 PM set, which is copied after midnight, and the day's 11 AM set.
    • Publishes nightly Railroad Commission of Texas, Completion Information in Data Format. A zip per day of completion forms (W-2, G-1, P-4, L-1, G-5, G-10, P-15, W-12) submitted or approved that day: counts by district, county and operator, and the best oil tests. The RRC posts a day's zip at about 2:20 the next morning, so a report shows the day before's.
    • Publishes nightly Drilling Permit (W1) Imaged Files, Directional Survey Applications and Imaged Completion Files. Zips of PDF and TIFF images of scanned filings. The size of each zip is read for every day, as a rough sign of how busy it was. For days captured live the zip is also opened: documents are counted by kind, and page one of a spread of them is shown shrunk and blurred so that nothing can be read, because the filings carry lease names, well numbers and the names of individuals, which the site's own pages leave out. The exceptions, written in the README (Privacy): every document of the three packs is published unmodified and readable as a file of a GitHub release, each under the well (API number) it is about on the Texas well images page, with a picture of its first page; and the Texas drill pages link to the W-1 letters, plats and directional surveys of their day. The Commission has no address that opens a single document, so they are kept here.
    • The Texas capture keeps 92 days. Days before the first nightly capture were rebuilt from the RRC's own file history by the same rule (month-end permit files, and the completion zips, image packs and pending sets that had landed on the share each day) and are marked as reconstructed. Operators are named only when they are corporations, LLCs or limited partnerships; lease names and well numbers are never shown.

    Nightly capture: Oklahoma

    • Publishes daily Oklahoma Corporation Commission, Oil & Gas Data: ITD-wells-formations-daily.xlsx (intents to drill approved in the last 7 complete days), completions-wells-formations-daily.xlsx (completion filings, Form 1002A) and well-transfers-daily.xlsx (wells that changed operator, about a month of them). The Daily report shows what each held, one day at a time, beside Texas (or on its own with Show set to Oklahoma): counts by county, kind, planned depth and operator. The Commission makes these files at about 7:40 PM Central and they end the day before, so a report shows only whole days (Monday's shows Friday's) and fills any gap from the Commission's master files.
    • Publishes nightly The same page's well file rbdms-wells.csv (about 457,000 well records, 121 MB): counted by status, kind, county and operator and never listed, on the days a report was captured live.
    • The master files ITD-wells-formations-base.xlsx (since 1999) and completions-wells-formations-base.xlsx (since 2010) rebuilt the days before the first capture, which are marked as reconstructed; the report keeps 92 days. The permit file does not say which permits are horizontal, so planned depth of 12,000 feet or more stands in. Operators are named only when they are corporations, LLCs or limited partnerships. Lease and well names, well and API numbers, and the operator, purchaser and plugger contact lists the Commission also publishes are never used.
    • Publishes daily The same master permit workbook, read once for the Companies page and the Daily report’s rings: new-drill permits by approval month since 2019, by county for the last 12 months and the 12 before, the operators with a permit in the last 24 months (named by the same corporate rule), and one point for each new-drill permit of the last 24 months at the surface location the Commission records, rounded to about 100 meters. A permit is one well and one approval day (the master repeats a permit once per formation until mid-2025), so a month's total matches the Daily report's. Nearly all of the last 24 months' permits have a location. The operators are those with a permit, not every registered operator: the Commission's operator list holds names, phone numbers and addresses and is not used.
    • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration, Oklahoma field production of crude oil (thousand barrels a month, series MCRFPOK1, through ), beside the WTI and Henry Hub prices listed above, in the Daily report's monthly figures. The newest months are EIA estimates that get revised. That card is not for the report's day.
    • U.S. Census Bureau, cartographic boundary files. The 77 Oklahoma county outlines (2025, 1:5,000,000), for the Oklahoma map.

    Nightly capture: New Mexico

    • Publishes daily New Mexico Oil Conservation Division, Oil and Gas Wells, the map-service copy of the well layer (OCDView/API_Export), which adds each well's effective_date: for a well still in status New, the day its permit to drill was approved. The Division publishes no daily permit file, so a day's report is the wells approved on the newest day before it (Monday shows Friday), counted once each by county, operator, kind of well, direction (horizontal, vertical, directional) and district. Planned depth, filing dates, amendments, completions and transfers are not published by day, so the report leaves them out. The layer is entered some days after an approval, so the newest two or three days run low and fill in; the pace chart is rewritten from the layer each night. A rebuilt day counts the approvals still in status New. The report keeps 92 days. For days captured live it also counts the whole layer (about 142,000 wells) by status and, for the active ones, by kind, county and operator, as Oklahoma's well inventory does; nothing is listed.

    New Mexico

    • Publishes daily New Mexico Oil Conservation Division, Oil and Gas Wells, the well layer of the Division's Geospatial Hub (about 142,000 wells, refreshed daily). Read for the Overview, the Companies page and the Daily report’s rings as wells spudded by month since 2019, by county for the last 12 months and the 12 before, the operators that spudded a well in the last 24 months (named by the same corporate rule as the other states) and one point per well at its surface location, rounded to about 100 meters. It counts spuds, not permits: the Division publishes no permit approval date by well, and the layer's history cannot say which wells were added. Spud dates are reported about two months late, so the figures end 60 days back (). The layer has a depth for only a few new wells, so no depth is shown, and no office city or state.
    • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration, New Mexico field production of crude oil (thousand barrels a month, series MCRFPNM1, through ). The newest months are EIA estimates that get revised. The Division's own monthly total for August agrees within 3%.
    • U.S. Census Bureau, cartographic boundary files. The 33 New Mexico county outlines (2025, 1:5,000,000).

    Federal offshore

    • Publishes weekly Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement, Data Center: all releasable borehole information (layout; about 55,000 boreholes, made every Sunday). Read for the Overview, the Companies page and the Daily report’s rings as wells spudded in the federal waters of the Gulf of America by month since , by protraction area for the last 12 months and the 12 before, the operators that spudded a well in the last 24 months (named by the same corporate rule as the other states) and one point per well at its surface location, rounded to about 100 meters. A well is the first ten digits of its API number, on the spud day of its earliest borehole. A sidetrack or bypass is drilling too, but it is not another well and is not counted, so an operator that mostly sidetracks old wells can show none. It counts spuds, not permits: BSEE publishes approvals to drill only as an online query, not as a file. Spud dates arrive late, so the figures end 60 days back (); how late BSEE's own are has not been measured yet. The file's total depth arrives after a well is finished, months later, so it covers only a few of the newest wells and no depth is shown, and no office city or state.
    • Publishes monthly The Data Center's company file (the numbers and names of the companies BSEE knows), read only to name an operator number. Its addresses and contacts are never read.
    • Publishes monthly U.S. Energy Information Administration, Federal Offshore--Gulf of America field production of crude oil (thousand barrels a month, series MCRFP3FM1, through ), the offshore's one figure of production here (). The newest months are EIA estimates that get revised. BSEE's own production files are not used.
    • The Data Center's protraction diagrams (protclip.zip, the official diagrams of the Gulf clipped to federal waters). The 63 protraction areas, such as Green Canyon, Mississippi Canyon and Eugene Island, are the offshore's "counties" on the maps, simplified to about a kilometer. Many have no wells: the Eastern Gulf is closed to leasing.

    Fractured wells with a GPS point (the Overview)

    • Publishes daily; read here daily FracFocus Chemical Disclosure Registry, the national hydraulic-fracturing disclosure list (public, run by the Ground Water Protection Council and the Interstate Oil and Gas Compact Commission). Only its disclosure list is read (about 252,000 rows, one per fractured well and job, 16 MB of a 443 MB download, fetched with range requests): the well's latitude and longitude, state, county, the day the job started, true vertical depth, water used, whether it is on federal or tribal land, and the operator. Every well since 2011 with a usable point is drawn (the last two years load with the page, the earlier ones, about 2 MB, when “Since 2011” is chosen). Each day's download is also compared with the list of wells kept from the last one (kept out of the repository, never published) to find the wells that are new, and those a Texas daily report had already carried are counted apart (Oklahoma's and New Mexico's daily reports list no wells, so they cannot be matched): the Daily report's “New in the registry” card shows the counts beside the oil well permits each state agency approved, marked, never added. The API number, the well's name and the lease are never kept; an operator is named only when it is a corporation, LLC or partnership (or one of a short reviewed list of companies whose registered names carry no “Inc”).
    • A well counts once, on the day of its first job. Operators disclose weeks to months after a job, so a month counts as settled only 120 days after the newest job, the 12-month figures use settled months only, and the newest months are drawn lighter. The registry lists wells that were fractured, which is nearly every new oil well but not every permit: a state with few fractured wells has few or none here, and says so. These counts are never added to the permit counts beside them on the Overview, which are by the agencies’ own approval dates.
    • U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files (1:10,000,000, public domain, as packaged in the us-atlas package): the outlines of the 49 states that can hold oil, simplified to about a kilometer, drawn on the Overview. A well on the border can sit a pixel over it.

    Method

    • Operators: all active P-5 registrants, narrowed to the that are corporations, LLCs or limited partnerships.
    • Companies: entities filed under an oil and gas NAICS code, plus active entities with no code whose names contain an oil and gas word such as oilfield, petroleum or wireline. Name-only matches on drilling, pipeline and well service never rate better than check.
    • RRC matching is by normalized company name, then by name prefix, then by a similar name in the same ZIP with the same leading letters and numerals.
    • New-drill permits are application type 01, counted once per permit number. "Last 12 months" counts run from October 29, 2025 to the newest permit in the RRC file, which is why they cover fewer days than the prior 12 months. Trends and the map's change view therefore compare complete calendar months instead: the last 12 against the 12 before.
    • Production change compares the latest 12 complete months of oil with the 12 before (for example 12 months to January 2026 against the 12 before). Production figures for counties and operators add up reported lease volumes; an operator's figures are for the leases it operates.
    • Basin cards and the basin outline on the map use the county lists above. The operators named on a basin card come from each county's top-eight operator list, so they are the operators with the most permits there, not a full ranking.
    • Companies by county use the Comptroller's county code, which numbers counties alphabetically (not in FIPS order); 107 Texas rows with code 0 are left out of the county counts. The County filter on the Texas directory uses the county that holds each ZIP code's center, so its counts can differ from the map's (within about 2% for most counties, more for a few such as Midland).
    • Companies by ZIP: 1,560 ZIP codes cover every pooled Texas company. 239 ZIPs have no Census center (PO boxes and the like) and are placed at their city's center, so several ZIPs of one city can share a point.
    • The Overview’s permit counts are each agency’s own count of every permit it approved (or well spudded). Only the Daily report’s rings use the located subset (Oklahoma, New Mexico and the federal offshore publish a location for nearly all of theirs).
    • The Overview ranks states, counties and operators from the new-wells files and the agencies’ permit file in your browser. A change on the 12 months before is shown only from a base of 20 wells or more; a smaller one reads n/a.

    Limits

    • The Overview counts fractured wells, not permits. FracFocus lists a well when its operator discloses the fracturing job, weeks to months after it (a month is about 94% complete after four months), so the Overview’s figures end in the last settled month and its newest months fill in later. A well that is not fractured (a vertical, injection or service well) is not in it, and states whose wells are mostly not fractured (Alabama, Mississippi, Illinois, Michigan, California, New York and others) show few or none; the permits of every state with a source are on the same page, beside the wells in the map, the ranking and the month chart.
    • Series end on different dates. Permits run to October 7, 2026, rig counts to August 28, EIA Texas crude to July, tax receipts to August, jobs and prices to September, and the EIA outlook is a forecast to December 2027. Read each chip and chart against its own date.
    • About 80% of Comptroller entities have no NAICS code. Service companies with generic names, no code and no RRC registration are not in the pool.
    • Permits measure activity, not budget or need.
    • New Mexico and the federal offshore count something else. Texas and Oklahoma publish permits by approval date; New Mexico and BSEE do not, so their figures are wells spudded, which come about two months after the permit and arrive late. The Overview adds permits only to permits, names their spuds wherever it shows them, and ends their series two months back. Neither has a depth, rig counts, jobs, taxes, companies or production by county here.
    • The federal offshore is small. wells in the last 12 months, against thousands of permits a year in Texas, so a month holds only a handful and one more well is a large swing: read the 12-month figures. Many areas have none, and a rank among operators moves with one well. Its file is weekly, so there is no Daily report for it.
    • Oklahoma is small beside Texas. The Commission approves about 3 new-drill permits a business day (Texas about 31), so single days swing: read the 20-day average. Its permit file does not say which wells are horizontal, it has no pending queue or image packs, its transfer file reaches back only about a month, and the well inventory is counted only on days captured live. Oklahoma jobs and rig counts are not shown: their sources could not be reached when the site was built. Beside the permits, its Overview has operators, active wells and crude production, and no company pool, taxes or production by county.
    • Permit rings in the Daily report are a located subset. The RRC publishes a surface location for 70% of new-drill permits in the last 24 months but only 40% of the last 12, and the gaps are uneven: January 2026 and April to October 2026 are thin, and the busiest counties (Midland, Reeves, Upton, Martin) have almost no rings. Do not read ring density as activity; read the counts, which the Daily report gives for every permit approved that day.
    • A few Texas permits for wells in the Gulf (the state's own waters, RRC area codes 600 to 713) are in Texas's statewide totals but not in any county or dot. They are not the federal offshore's wells, which have a state of their own on this site.
    • Operator trend, spud, depth and the 24-month chart exist for the operators with a permit in the last 24 months in the four full files (Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico and the federal offshore), not for the operators listed for another state, which have the count of the last 12 months and nothing more. A blank cell means no data, never zero.
    • Production is older than permits. Oil and gas figures end at January 2026, the newest month the Railroad Commission's reports cover completely; permits run to October 2026. Oil and gas from operators that are not current P-5 registrants appear on the Overview only, tagged, since the Operators page lists active registrants.
    • BLS withholds county figures that could identify an employer: 52 counties have oil and gas figures and 101 have services figures, so most counties show "No data (suppressed by BLS)". County jobs figures are for 2025; the monthly jobs chart is statewide.
    • The EIA outlook is a model forecast and will be revised each month. Weekly rig counts after August 28, 2026 are not shown because they could not be read; they are missing, not zero.
    • Gas processing plants carry no capacity (no current public source), some entries are compressor or fractionation sites inside a plant, and facilities built after 2023 are missing. Port Arthur LNG and Rio Grande LNG are under construction and not shown.
    • Fit flags are rules applied to names and addresses, not research. Verify before you reach out.
    • Matching has been spot-checked, not audited.

    How to use the site

    How to use this site, and what the columns mean

    How to use it

    • Companies has two lists behind one switch. Operators is national: every operator the state agencies name, ranked by new wells in the last 12 months (Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico and the federal offshore list every operator with a recent permit or spud; for the other states, every operator the state agency named in the last 12 months, with its count: a person trading under a name is never listed). Narrow it with the State filter and start with bands A and B: they are drilling now. Tick Top producers to see who produces the most oil in Texas. Texas directory lists the Comptroller pool: entities filed under an oil and gas industry code, plus entities with no code whose names say oil and gas. Where a company matched an RRC operator, its permit counts are filled in. No other state publishes a list like it, so the directory is Texas only.
    • Overview: oil permits and fractured wells on one page, each marked the same way everywhere (a hollow ring, an outlined bar or a line is a state agency’s oil permits; a filled dot or bar is a fractured well; the two are never added). Hover a dot for the well, hover a state or ring for both counts, click a state to zoom in and read both for it, press Watch to replay two years of fractured wells, choose Since 2011 to draw and replay every well in the registry, or switch the map to the wells or the permits alone. Below the map: the states ranked by either count, the months as lines over bars, the busiest counties and top operators from both sources, and who is covered.
    • Share this view on both lists copies a link that reopens exactly what you see. Download CSV exports the rows you filtered, public fields only.

    Reading the columns

    Band A, B, C
    Drilling activity from RRC permits. A: 10 or more new-well permits in the last 12 months. B: 1 to 9. C: an RRC operator with none. Companies with no RRC match show a dash.
    Trend
    New-drill permits in the last 12 full calendar months against the 12 before. "new" means none in the prior 12. Blank means the operator has no permit in the last 24 months.
    Oil, gas, production trend
    Crude oil plus condensate (barrels) and gas (mcf) reported to the RRC over the latest 12 months, and the change against the 12 months before. Blank means no production figure for that operator, not zero.
    Spud, depth
    Spud counts new-drill permits whose reported spud date falls in the last 12 months. Depth is the mean reported total depth of the operator's permits over 24 months.
    Fit
    Likely is an active entity with no warning signs. Check means verify first: a mineral or royalty name, six or more entities at one address, a weak name match (drilling, pipeline, well service), or a fuel wholesaler.
    New entity
    Chartered with the Texas Secretary of State in the last 24 months.
    New to drilling, New P-5
    First drilling permit ever filed, or first RRC registration, in the last 24 months.
    RRC match
    How a company was tied to an RRC operator: exact name, name prefix (RRC names are cut at 32 characters), or a similar name in the same ZIP, which is the least reliable.
    New wells, permits
    Permits are counted once per permit number. New wells counts new-drill applications only; recompletions and re-entries count under permits.

    What is not on this site

    Officer names, phone numbers and street addresses stay in the CSV files, not here. Sole proprietors and other non-entity registrants, trust and family names, and deprioritized names are left out.

    • FERC, PHMSA and EPA ECHO: pipelines, power plants and large industrial gas users.
